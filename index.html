Enable your location

Allow location access to show your blue dot. If access is blocked, change your browser permissions, then try again.

iPhone or iPad
In Safari, open the Page Menu → Website Settings → Location and choose Ask or Allow. Also check Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services for your browser.

Android Chrome
Tap the site information icon beside the address → Permissions → Location and allow access. Check that your phone’s location is on too.

Other browsers
Open the site permissions beside the address and allow Location.

About This App

This is an unofficial NYC Ferry planner, built out of frustration with a specific problem: the official app can't plan trips that require a transfer.

If your journey crosses more than one route, you're normally stuck cross-referencing timetables and working it out yourself. This app handles that automatically — just enter your origin and destination and it finds the best way to get there.

Built by ferry riders for ferry riders.

You Asked For It: Nerd Mode

Vanilla ES modules plan your trip in the browser. A small relay connects the official live ferry feeds to this app.

Routing searches actual ferry departures and connections across the timetable. Depart-at searches find the earliest arrival; arrive-by searches find the latest departure that meets your deadline. When departure and arrival times match, fewer transfers win. Transfer buffers apply when changing ferries, and the map and directions use the stops served by your selected trip.

Map lines use separate lanes where routes share a corridor, with constant pixel spacing as you zoom. Rounded joins keep bends tidy, and the lines meet at their actual landings. Selected trips use the same geometry as the network map.

Three options (Earlier / Best / Later) come from exact scheduled departures across the selected date. Trips overtaken by later ferries are removed, and navigation follows the remaining journeys without sampling times.

Data is pulled from NYC Ferry's public GTFS feed and baked into a single JSON file. The timetable refreshes daily. Live positions, trip predictions, and service alerts are checked every 30 seconds; stale reports expire automatically.

The whole thing was prototyped with three AI coding agents — Codex, Claude Code, and Mistral Vibe — competing on different approaches for the router, the UX, and the map rendering. The best ideas from each survived. The rest got git reset --hard.
